\noindent\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule}{\textbf{Claim.} The systematic error of universal machine-learning interatomic potentials is not uniform: across referenced measurements on halides, perovskites, and alloys, kinetic barriers survive to within $\sim$10\,\% while defect formation energies collapse by 40--105\,\% --- with cross-material orderings preserved even where magnitudes fail. This structure licenses a narrow, provably-safe correction (class-native lattice constants, validated out-of-sample at 2.4--5$\times$), exact-physics refusals (Born instability of entire ensemble hulls), and honest abstention everywhere else. Which of the three applies is decidable at runtime, and the safety conditions are machine-checked theorems.}}

\bigskip
\noindent Materials discovery for batteries, solid electrolytes, carbon capture, and lead-free photovoltaics is rate-limited by the same computational bottleneck: the properties that decide whether a candidate works --- vacancy formation, migration barriers, defect-mediated stability --- live exactly where universal machine-learning interatomic potentials (uMLIPs) are least trained and least accurate. The field knows these models soften the potential-energy surface at under-coordinated sites. What has been missing is resolution: \emph{which} observables fail, by \emph{how much} against real references, whether the failure has exploitable structure, and --- most practically --- a decision procedure that tells a screening pipeline when to trust a prediction, when to repair it, and when to throw it away. This paper reports referenced measurements that answer the first three questions and a validated, theorem-guarded procedure for the fourth.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- instrument
\section*{The instrument}

All measurements share one probe. A deterministic statics core (lattice relaxation by Birch--Murnaghan energy--volume scan; relaxed-ion stress--strain elastic constants at $\pm 0.5\,\%$ strain; Born stability as exact arithmetic) extends to defect formation (referenced vacancies, charge-balanced Schottky pairs), to kinetics (climbing-image NEB with relaxed endpoints), and to random solid solutions (exact-count, seed-pinned supercells). Four foundation potentials --- CHGNet 0.4.2 and three MACE-MP variants --- run through identical code paths on identical cells; a classical LAMMPS leg (2NN-MEAM, native binary driven through the Python module, verified against the standalone executable to $10^{-9}$~GPa) runs the \emph{same cells through the same probe functions} where classical potentials exist. Calibration baselines (21 metals, 6 ionic rocksalts, 5 perovskites, 4 fcc alloys) are measured by the same instrument that gates subjects, so thresholds never inherit a probe mismatch. Reproducibility: a full independent rerun reproduces every verdict, with worst-case raw-value drift of $8\times 10^{-4}$ (GPU float nondeterminism through finite differences). Every reference value carries a primary citation, a kind (experimental / DFT / derived), and a convention caveat; unfindable values are null, never estimated.

\begin{definition}[Born stability, cubic --- exact physics]\label{def:born}
A cubic elastic triple $(C_{11}, C_{12}, C_{44})$ is \emph{Born-stable} if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:born}
C_{11} - C_{12} > 0, \qquad C_{11} + 2\,C_{12} > 0, \qquad C_{44} > 0,
\end{equation}
the necessary mechanical-stability conditions of a cubic lattice under zero external stress --- tetragonal-shear, volumetric, and trigonal-shear stability respectively (M.~Born \& K.~Huang, \emph{Dynamical Theory of Crystal Lattices}, 1954; F.~Mouhat \& F.-X.~Coudert, \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ B} \textbf{90}, 224104 (2014)). A model whose predicted constants fail \eqref{eq:born} describes a mechanically unstable phase --- grounds for refusal regardless of accuracy elsewhere.
\end{definition}

\section*{Result 1 --- The softening dichotomy}

The sharpest result of this program is a split the softening literature has not resolved. On five rocksalt halides with citation-locked experimental references:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Kinetic saddle points largely survive.} Cation-vacancy migration barriers from CI-NEB land close to both experiment and published DFT. For LiF, the four models span \textbf{0.48--0.68~eV} against an experimental migration enthalpy of \textbf{0.66\,$\pm$\,0.03~eV} (Stoebe \& Huggins 1966) and neutral PBE-NEB values of \textbf{0.66--0.73~eV}; the best MACE variants sit within $\sim$5\,\% at the working supercell and within $\sim$10--14\,\% after finite-size correction (a $3\times3\times3$ convergence study bounds the supercell error at \textbf{12--50~meV} --- three to eight times smaller than the cross-model spread, so model disagreement is the dominant uncertainty, not the cell).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Defect formation energetics collapse.} Schottky pair enthalpies --- referenced against the conductivity-derived experimental values $h_{\mathrm S} = 2(E_{\mathrm{intrinsic}} - \Delta H_{\mathrm m})$ for LiF (2.42~eV), LiCl (2.12), LiBr (1.80), LiI (1.34), and NaCl (2.83) --- are underestimated by \textbf{40--105\,\% by every model}. The best model reaches barely 60\,\% of the experimental value on its best compound; the worst predicts \emph{spontaneous} vacancy-pair formation in LiI ($-0.08$~eV) --- a crystal that disassembles itself. The same pathology recurs in the lead-free perovskite class: one model predicts negative B-site vacancy formation in CsGeI$_3$, and cross-model spreads on neutral vacancy formation run \textbf{0.5--1.8~eV}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Yet the ordering survives the collapse.} All four models rank the Schottky series LiF $>$ LiCl $>$ LiBr $>$ LiI in the experimental order, even while every magnitude is wrong by half. The size is wrong; the order is right --- now measured on defect thermodynamics with experimental anchors, not inferred.

\medskip
The mechanistic reading is direct. Along a migration path, coordination is approximately conserved: the hopping ion trades neighbors but the saddle configuration is only mildly under-coordinated relative to the minimum, so the systematic softening largely cancels in the barrier \emph{difference}. Defect formation, by contrast, \emph{is} coordination loss --- its energy is dominated by exactly the under-coordinated environments the training distributions lack --- so the softening appears at full strength. This resolves an apparent tension in screening practice: kinetics-limited screens (conductivity, diffusivity) can tolerate raw uMLIPs far better than stability-limited screens (defect tolerance, degradation), and the difference is not noise but mechanism. At the standard conversion of 100~meV $\approx$ 50$\times$ in rate at room temperature, however, even the surviving barrier spreads of \textbf{0.13--0.20~eV} correspond to two to three orders of magnitude of disagreement in predicted ionic conductivity from model choice alone --- kinetic screening tolerates raw uMLIPs only under cross-model concordance, not on any single model's word.

\section*{Result 2 --- Disagreement has structure, but it is not a universal uncertainty signal}

Cross-model dispersion is the cheapest reference-free signal a screening pipeline can compute, and an influential critique (PROBE) reports that raw ensemble disagreement often correlates weakly with true error. Our reference-bound corpora let us measure exactly where that critique lands. The dispersion--error rank correlation is strongly property- and class-dependent: stacking-fault energy $\rho = 0.89$, vacancy formation 0.61, lattice constants 0.57 pooled --- but bulk modulus \textbf{0.16} pooled and {\boldmath$-0.63$} within fcc metals: for that class--property pair, \emph{higher} disagreement weakly predicts \emph{lower} error. Dispersion gating is an uncertainty statement for some (class, property) pairs and a normality test for others, and nothing in the signal itself tells you which.

\begin{definition}[Relative cross-model dispersion; metrics unfloored and \code{floored-v1}]\label{def:dispersion}
For model predictions $v_1,\dots,v_K$ ($K \ge 2$) of one property on one material, the \emph{relative cross-model dispersion} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dispersion}
d(v_1,\dots,v_K) \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\max_k v_k \;-\; \min_k v_k}{\bigl|\operatorname{median}_k v_k\bigr|}\,,
\end{equation}
undefined (it explodes) when the cross-model median crosses zero. The registered Round-3 instrument fix (metric \code{floored-v1}) floors the denominator at a class-median scale: for materials $i = 1,\dots,N$ of one class, with per-material cross-model median $m_i$ and spread $\mathrm{sp}_i = \max_k v_k^{(i)} - \min_k v_k^{(i)}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:floored}
d_i^{\mathrm{floored}} \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{sp}_i}{\max\bigl(|m_i|,\; \varphi\, M\bigr)}\,,
\qquad
M = \operatorname{median}_i |m_i|,
\qquad
\varphi = 0.1 .
\end{equation}
Cells with $|m_i| \ge \varphi M$ are unchanged from \eqref{eq:dispersion}; sign-crossing cells become finite and comparable.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Thresholds are measured, never invented]\label{rem:thresholds}
Concordance flag/refuse thresholds are the $p75$/$p95$ percentiles of a \emph{measured} baseline distribution of \eqref{eq:dispersion} or \eqref{eq:floored} over at least five calibration materials, with the source recorded in every verdict. A dispersion strictly below the flag threshold is \emph{concordant}, at-or-above flag but strictly below refuse is \emph{flagged}, and at-or-beyond refuse is \emph{refused}; the three zones are mutually exclusive and exhaustive for every well-formed window (Lean \code{outcome\_trichotomy}), and threshold migration is law-governed: tightening can only add refusals, and any un-refusal certifies a strictly looser threshold (Lean \code{refused\_stable\_under\_tightening}, \code{unrefusal\_needs\_looser\_threshold}).
\end{remark}
\artifacts{\code{lupine\_distill.statics.gates.relative\_dispersion}, \code{dispersions\_by\_material\_floored} (registered constants \code{DEFAULT\_DISPERSION\_FLOOR\_FRACTION} $= 0.1$, metric id \code{DISPERSION\_METRIC\_FLOORED\_V1}), \code{derive\_concordance\_thresholds}; Lean \code{Lupine.Shapes.ConcordanceWindow} and the threshold-migration laws.}

Our response is architectural rather than rhetorical: every gate in the pipeline now carries a \textbf{license} --- \code{licensed}, \code{descriptive}, or \code{anti-correlated} --- assigned per (class, property) from reference-bound data under a fixed rule, fail-closed, and updated only from calibration corpora, never from gated subjects. Under the current registry exactly one dispersion gate is licensed as an uncertainty signal (bcc lattice constants, $\rho = 0.89$, $n = 7$); bulk-modulus concordance is descriptive program-wide. Refusals that do not depend on any license do the heavy lifting: Born instability is exact physics, and per-model Born failures compose into ensemble statements --- if the axis-aligned hull of all models' elastic predictions violates a Born inequality at its favourable endpoint, \emph{every} tensor in the hull is unstable, a machine-checked impossibility certificate that refuses a candidate for the whole ensemble range at once. In our panels these exact-physics refusals correctly rejected a rocksalt LiS hypothesis (all four models: $C_{44} < 0$) and, defensibly, cubic CsPbI$_3$ at 0~K --- while per-property calibrated thresholds resolved the false refusals that a single-property proxy had inflicted on known-good subjects (Li$_2$S antifluorite and the halide electrolyte panel: from 8/8 refused to 4 certified, 2 flagged, 2 marginal under class-native calibration). Because refusal is cheap and final --- no later gate can overturn it, a theorem --- the pipeline runs its costliest probe only on survivors, cutting panel wall time by 16--42\,\% with verdicts unchanged; the savings concentrate precisely on the worst physics, since dynamically unstable candidates have the most expensive relaxations.

\begin{definition}[Gate-license rule --- fail-closed]\label{def:license}
For one (class, property) concordance channel with Spearman dispersion--error rank correlation $\rho$ measured on $n$ calibration materials of corpus kind $\kappa$, the license status is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:license}
\operatorname{status}(\rho, n, \kappa) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\text{\code{anti-correlated}} & \text{if } \rho \le -0.5,\ n \ge 5,\\
& \quad \kappa = \text{reference-bound},\\[3pt]
\text{\code{licensed}} & \text{if } \rho \ge +0.5,\ n \ge 5,\ \kappa = \text{reference-bound},\\
& \quad \text{and no program \code{license\_ceiling} applies},\\[3pt]
\text{\code{descriptive}} & \text{otherwise --- the fail-closed default.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A license annotates; it never re-gates. A (class, property) pair absent from the registry --- or an absent registry --- yields \code{descriptive}; a program ceiling caps positive licensing only and can never erase an \code{anti-correlated} warning; \code{licensed} means low dispersion may be read as empirically lower error within the recorded $(\rho, n)$, \code{descriptive} means agreement arithmetic only with no uncertainty claim, and \code{anti-correlated} means low dispersion must \emph{not} be read as low error.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ensemble elastic hull]\label{def:hull}
For an ensemble of models $j = 1,\dots,J$ with cubic elastic predictions $\bigl(C_{11}^{(j)}, C_{12}^{(j)}, C_{44}^{(j)}\bigr)$, the \emph{axis-aligned interval hull} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hull}
H \;=\; I_{11} \times I_{12} \times I_{44},
\qquad
I_{ab} \;=\; \bigl[\,C_{ab}^{\min},\ C_{ab}^{\max}\,\bigr],
\end{equation}
with the interval endpoints the component-wise minima and maxima over the models; $e \in H$ means each constant of $e$ lies inside its interval (Lean \code{memHull}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Ensemble-hull Born refusal; Lean \code{hull\_born\_refusal\_c44}, \code{hull\_born\_\allowbreak refusal\_shear}, \code{hull\_born\_\allowbreak refusal\_volumetric}]\label{thm:hull}
Let $H$ be an ensemble elastic hull. If any one of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hullrefusal}
\mathrm{(i)}\ \ C_{44}^{\max} \le 0,
\qquad
\mathrm{(ii)}\ \ C_{11}^{\max} - C_{12}^{\min} \le 0,
\qquad
\mathrm{(iii)}\ \ C_{11}^{\max} + 2\,C_{12}^{\max} \le 0
\end{equation}
holds --- that is, the hull violates one Born inequality of \eqref{eq:born} at its most favourable endpoint --- then \emph{every} tensor in the hull is Born-unstable:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hullconclusion}
\forall\, e \in H:\quad \neg\,\operatorname{bornStable}(e).
\end{equation}
The refusal holds for the whole ensemble range, not just the sampled models.
\end{theorem}

There is also a hierarchy worth stating plainly: disagreement grows monotonically along bulk $\to$ kinetic $\to$ defect observables, in both relative and absolute terms (defect-energy disagreement runs 2--34$\times$ the bulk-modulus disagreement on the same compounds). The observables that matter most for discovery are the ones the models agree on least --- which is the quantitative case for why screening pipelines need verdicts, not just predictions.

\section*{Result 3 --- A correction with a validated scope and machine-checked safety}

Can any of this be repaired rather than refused? We tested the simplest defensible repair --- a multiplicative de-bias learned from same-class reference-bound calibration, applied only when direction evidence is unanimous --- through three preregistered stages, and the outcome is a correction whose scope we can state exactly.

Transferring biases \emph{across} chemical classes fails, and fails provably: a bias learned on elemental fcc metals (which underpredict bulk moduli) applied to fcc alloys (which overpredict) \emph{worsened} alloy predictions from 9.1\,\% to 16.9\,\% median error. That failure is now a theorem rather than an anecdote --- a correction applied against the target's true error direction strictly increases error --- and its converse is the safety condition we prove: if all calibration ratios lie strictly on one side of unity, the bias and the target's ratio lie in the calibration hull, and the bias clears a margin cap, then the corrected error is \emph{strictly smaller} than the raw error. The cap is honestly asymmetric: inflation-side corrections are safe when the bias exceeds twice the calibration spread ($2s$); deflation-side corrections require three times the spread \emph{and} a floor on the bias (the mirrored $2s$ condition has a kernel-checked counterexample). These are decidable integer statements, machine-checked, with the one harmful correction our exploratory round produced refused by the theorem on two independent grounds.

\begin{theorem}[Wrong-direction corrections strictly harm; Lean \code{wrong\_direction\_\allowbreak inflation\_worsens}, \code{wrong\_direction\_\allowbreak deflation\_worsens}]\label{thm:direction}
Let $\mathrm{pred}$, $\mathrm{ref}$, $\mathrm{corrected}$ be integers (predictions and reference in the evidence corpus's fixed-point scaling). Quoting the kernel-checked hypotheses exactly:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textup{(i)}] \textup{(inflation)} if $\mathrm{ref} \le \mathrm{pred}$ \textup{(\code{hover})} and $\mathrm{pred} < \mathrm{corrected}$ \textup{(\code{hinc})}, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wrongdir}
\bigl|\mathrm{pred} - \mathrm{ref}\bigr| \;<\; \bigl|\mathrm{corrected} - \mathrm{ref}\bigr| ;
\end{equation}
\item[\textup{(ii)}] \textup{(deflation)} if $\mathrm{pred} \le \mathrm{ref}$ \textup{(\code{hunder})} and $\mathrm{corrected} < \mathrm{pred}$ \textup{(\code{hdec})}, then likewise
$\bigl|\mathrm{pred} - \mathrm{ref}\bigr| < \bigl|\mathrm{corrected} - \mathrm{ref}\bigr|$.
\end{itemize}
Inflating a prediction already at-or-above its reference, or deflating one already at-or-below it, strictly increases the error. Without an in-class direction witness the correction must abstain ($\mathrm{corrected} := \mathrm{pred}$), which never changes the error (Lean \code{CorrectionAnchor}, \code{directionVerified}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{rulealg}[Frozen Round-3 correction rule --- registered 2026-07-13, before reference sourcing]\label{rule:frozen}
For a held-out candidate $X$, model $m$, property $p$, class $C$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Calibration set} $=$ the other class-$C$ members with a non-null reference (never $X$). Require $\ge 2$ members, else \textsc{abstain}.
\item \emph{Ratios:}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratios}
r_i \;=\; \mathrm{pred}_i / \mathrm{ref}_i \quad \text{over calibration members } i .
\end{equation}
\item \emph{Direction gate (unanimity):} apply only if all ratios are strictly on one side of unity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unanimity}
\bigl(\forall i:\ r_i > 1\bigr) \quad\text{or}\quad \bigl(\forall i:\ r_i < 1\bigr),
\end{equation}
else \textsc{abstain}.
\item \emph{Magnitude cap:} with bias and spread
\begin{equation}\label{eq:biasspread}
b \;=\; \operatorname{median}_i r_i ,
\qquad
s \;=\; \max_i r_i - \min_i r_i ,
\end{equation}
\textsc{abstain} unless $|b - 1| > s$ --- the learned bias must exceed the calibration scatter.
\item \emph{Correction:}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected}
\mathrm{corrected} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{pred}/b & \text{if every gate above passes},\\[2pt]
\mathrm{pred} & \text{otherwise (abstention, risk-free).}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{rulealg}

\begin{theorem}[Capped in-hull correction helps, inflation side; Lean \code{capped\_inhull\_\allowbreak correction\_\allowbreak helps\_inflation}]\label{thm:capinf}
Let $\ell, h, s, b, r \in \mathbb{Z}$ be the Lean variables \code{lo}, \code{hi}, \code{s}, \code{b}, \code{r}: pred/ref ratios in the corpus scaling ($\times 10^4$, so the integer $10^4$ denotes the ratio $1$), with $[\ell, h]$ the calibration ratio hull, $b$ the bias, and $r$ the target's true ratio. Assume, quoting the kernel-checked hypotheses exactly:
\begin{align}
& s = h - \ell, && \text{\small\textup{(\code{hs}: the spread)}} \label{eq:capinf-hyp}\\
& 10^4 < \ell, \quad \ell \le h, && \text{\small\textup{(\code{\_hlo}, \code{hle}: hull strictly above unity)}} \nonumber\\
& \ell \le b \le h, && \text{\small\textup{(\code{hb1}, \code{hb2}: bias in-hull)}} \nonumber\\
& \ell \le r \le h, && \text{\small\textup{(\code{hr1}, \code{hr2}: target ratio in-hull)}} \nonumber\\
& 2s < b - 10^4 . && \text{\small\textup{(\code{hcap}: the cap $2s < b - 1$)}} \nonumber
\end{align}
The in-hull hypothesis on $r$ (\code{hr1}, \code{hr2}) is the formal content of the informal word ``in-class'': the target's true ratio behaves like its classmates'. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capinf-concl}
\bigl|r - b\bigr| \cdot 10^4 \;<\; \bigl|r - 10^4\bigr| \cdot b ,
\end{equation}
the division-free form of $\bigl|\,r/b - 1\,\bigr| < \bigl|\,r - 1\,\bigr|$ (equivalent for $b > 0$): dividing the prediction by the bias strictly shrinks the target's error.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Capped in-hull correction helps, deflation side; Lean \code{capped\_inhull\_\allowbreak correction\_\allowbreak helps\_deflation}]\label{thm:capdef}
With the same notation, assume, quoting the kernel-checked hypotheses exactly:
\begin{align}
& s = h - \ell, && \text{\small\textup{(\code{hs})}} \label{eq:capdef-hyp}\\
& 0 < \ell \le h < 10^4, && \text{\small\textup{(\code{\_hlo}, \code{\_hle}, \code{\_hhi}: hull strictly below unity)}} \nonumber\\
& \ell \le b \le h, \quad \ell \le r \le h, && \text{\small\textup{(\code{hb1}, \code{hb2}, \code{hr1}, \code{hr2}: bias and target in-hull)}} \nonumber\\
& 5000 \le b, && \text{\small\textup{(\code{hfloor}: the floor $b \ge 1/2$)}} \nonumber\\
& 3s < 10^4 - b . && \text{\small\textup{(\code{hcap}: the cap $3s < 1 - b$)}} \nonumber
\end{align}
Then the same conclusion \eqref{eq:capinf-concl} holds: $\bigl|r - b\bigr| \cdot 10^4 < \bigl|r - 10^4\bigr| \cdot b$, i.e.\ $\bigl|\,r/b - 1\,\bigr| < \bigl|\,r - 1\,\bigr|$. The deflation side is \emph{not} the mirror image of the inflation side: it needs three spreads and the floor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Asymmetry, counterexamples, and the frozen cap]\label{rem:asymmetry}
(i) In the Lean statements the underscored hypotheses (\code{\_hlo}, \code{\_hle}, \code{\_hhi}) pin the regime for the reader; they are derivable from the cap, the floor, and the memberships, and the proofs never use them. (ii) The asymmetry is derived, not assumed: the corrected error is measured relative to $b$; inflation ($b > 1$) divides by a larger denominator and gains margin, so $b - 1 > 2s$ suffices with no ceiling on $b$, while deflation ($b < 1$) divides by a smaller denominator, amplifying the same $|r - b|$, so the spread multiple rises to three and the floor $b \ge 1/2$ is needed. The mirrored $2s$ cap is insufficient below unity even with the floor in place: hull $[0.50, 0.74]$ ($s = 0.24$), $b = 0.50$, $r = 0.74$ passes the $2s$ cap yet gives corrected error $0.48$ against raw $0.26$ --- strictly worse (kernel-checked counterexample). (iii) The frozen Round-3 runtime cap $|b - 1| > s$ of Rule~\ref{rule:frozen} (one spread) is strictly weaker than the caps proved here: hull $[1.0001, 1.0003]$ ($s = 2\times10^{-4}$), $b = 1.0003$, $r = 1.0001$ passes the one-spread cap while the correction strictly harms (corrected error $\approx 2\times10^{-4}$ against raw $1\times10^{-4}$; kernel-checked). Round-3 results are therefore read under Round-3's own frozen registration, and the proven caps inform a registered Round-4 cap change only. (iv) The Round-2 FeNi $B_0$ cell that demanded these theorems fails \emph{both} license hypotheses: the target ratio $r = 0.810$ lies outside the calibration hull $[1.02, 1.175]$, and that hull cannot clear the inflation cap for any in-hull bias ($s = 0.155$ demands $b > 1.31$ while the hull tops out at $1.175$). The refusal is the theorem working as intended.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Exact two-sided binomial sign test]\label{def:signtest}
Over held-out cells where the rule \emph{applied}, a cell counts as improved when the corrected $|$relative error$|$ is strictly below the raw one, worsened when strictly above; ties are dropped. With $n = n_{\mathrm{improved}} + n_{\mathrm{worsened}}$, $k = n_{\mathrm{improved}}$, and $X \sim \operatorname{Bin}\bigl(n, \tfrac12\bigr)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signtest}
p \;=\; \min\Bigl\{\, 1,\; 2 \min\bigl( \Pr[X \le k],\ \Pr[X \ge k] \bigr) \Bigr\},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signtest-tails}
\Pr[X \le k] \;=\; 2^{-n} \sum_{i=0}^{k} \binom{n}{i},
\qquad
\Pr[X \ge k] \;=\; 2^{-n} \sum_{i=k}^{n} \binom{n}{i},
\end{equation}
computed exactly from integer binomial coefficients (no normal approximation, no continuity correction) and undefined at $n = 0$. The registered success criterion per group: corrected beats raw on $\ge 2/3$ of evaluable properties with $p < 0.1$.
\end{definition}

The confirmatory test used a rule frozen before its evaluation references were even sourced, on eight candidates never seen by any calibration: four alkali halides with primary-source elastic references and four out-of-sample perovskites. The result draws the scope line cleanly. \textbf{Class-native lattice-constant corrections transfer decisively: 1.60\,\% $\to$ 0.33\,\% median error on the ionics (4.8$\times$) and 1.75\,\% $\to$ 0.74\,\% on the perovskites (2.4$\times$), with the direction gate confident enough to fire on every cell.} Every other property failed the registered criteria --- mostly by \emph{abstention} (the gate correctly found no unanimous direction evidence on 12--16 of 16 cells), and where the frozen margin admitted a few applications on stiffness properties, they did not help, consistent with the proven caps being stronger than the frozen one. The registered consequence executed: the correction layer's validated public scope is \textbf{same-class lattice constants}, with abstention --- provably risk-free --- everywhere else until new evidence.

We regard this as a positive result, and the honest shape of the correction problem: lattice constants are where cross-model error direction is unanimous, biases are stable across a chemical class, and thermal-convention offsets are common-mode --- so a three-line, fully-auditable correction beats a 4-model ensemble at one quarter of its cost \emph{on that property}, while the same machinery declines to manufacture confidence anywhere it cannot prove it. Combined with Result 1, the division of labor for the five target classes is concrete: rankings and lattice geometry can be corrected; stability energetics must be gated and refused; kinetics demands concordance across independent architectures.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Independence, measured.} The multi-architecture cloud basis (SevenNet and Orb-v3 joining CHGNet and the MACE family; a sixth architecture awaits gated-checkpoint authorization) answers the redundancy question quantitatively. Pipeline first: 78/78 elastic predictions per architecture against the locked references, overall $C_{ij}$ MAE \textbf{9.0~GPa} (SevenNet) under a declared fixed-lattice protocol, total cloud cost \$0.23. The dispersion answer: \textbf{11 of 18} elastic rows widen when independent architectures join (median ratio 1.06$\times$), and the widening concentrates exactly where the local family looked most concordant --- the alloy's $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ dispersions grow \textbf{2.7--3.0$\times$} (the independent architectures place CoCrNi $C_{11}$ at 193--203~GPa against the family's 238--265) --- while $C_{44}$ rows, where CHGNet and MACE already spanned the extremes, are unchanged. Family agreement is not evidence of correctness; it is evidence of shared ancestry, and the license layer now has the measurement that says so.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The correction license cannot yet be checked without references --- measured.} We tested whether the theorems' load-bearing hypothesis (the target's error ratio lying in the calibration hull) has a reference-free proxy: is a model's \emph{prediction} inside its peers' prediction hull? The answer is a clean null --- success rates 70.8\,\% in-hull vs 71.4\,\% out (Fisher $p = 1.0$, 52 confirmatory cells, exclusion-corrected by independent blind re-derivation) --- while the unknowable oracle condition itself associates strongly (94.4\,\% vs 58.8\,\%, $p = 0.009$), and every cell that satisfied both the proven cap and the in-hull condition succeeded (\textbf{12/12 across all rounds} --- the theorem's guarantee holding empirically without exception). The theorem picks the right condition; nature declines to reveal it cheaply. Finding a knowable surrogate for hull membership is therefore the registered open problem of the correction program, and until one exists, the fail-closed answer stands: corrections carry the frozen gate, and the license layer reports what that gate cannot see.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- related work
\section*{Related work}

The components of this instrument have prior art; the assembled decision procedure does not, to our knowledge. Ensemble and committee disagreement as an uncertainty proxy is established for bespoke potentials and increasingly for foundation models; PROBE's demonstration that raw disagreement can correlate weakly with error is the direct challenge our license layer answers --- by measuring, per class and property, where the proxy holds, and refusing to issue uncertainty semantics where it does not. Conformal calibration supplies distribution-free per-quantity thresholds; our percentile gates are the same statistical object with measured, same-instrument provenance, plus a refusal-first cost ordering conformal methods do not address. Systematic uMLIP softening on under-coordinated configurations is documented (most directly for MPtrj-trained models); our contribution is its mechanistic resolution into a kinetics/thermodynamics dichotomy with experimental anchors on both sides, and the observation that ordering survives magnitude collapse on defect energetics. Proof-carrying certification of MLIP properties is nascent; our Lean layer differs in scope --- it certifies the \emph{decision procedure} (refusal finality, threshold-migration consequences, ensemble-hull impossibility, correction-safety caps) rather than the potential, which is what lets a screening pipeline inherit guarantees no single model can offer. Benchmark leaderboards (Matbench Discovery; elastic-property benchmarks showing bulk--shear accuracy asymmetry across $\sim 10^4$ materials) corroborate the property-resolved error structure but operate per-model; the object of study here is the ensemble-plus-verdict system.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- boundaries
\section*{Boundaries}

Panels of 4--9 compounds per class demonstrate methodology and mechanism, not survey statistics; every $n$ is stated per material, not per cell. Two of four local architectures share lineage, so dispersions understate independent error until the five-architecture basis lands. Neutral-defect conventions against charged-defect experimental references are stated per value; the Schottky comparison is enthalpy-consistent, and the barrier comparison is anchored on convention-consistent neutral DFT-NEB where it exists. Random-solid-solution cells are statistically cubic only; perovskite references for high-temperature phases meet athermal predictions, and part of the corrected lattice gain absorbs that common-mode offset --- the decomposition is registered work. The dispersion metric is undefined at sign-crossing medians and is floored accordingly (one vanadium shear cell in the calibration corpus is a documented pathology, not data). Licenses rest on small-$n$ rank correlations and are treated as fail-closed engineering, not statistics.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- outlook
\section*{Outlook}

Three lines follow directly. First, independence: the five-architecture cloud basis converts every dispersion in this paper from family spread toward independent-error spread at a cost of dollars, and its canary is running. Second, a runtime license for corrections: if prediction-hull membership --- knowable without references --- predicts correction success, the same fail-closed logic that licenses gates will license repairs, closing the loop between the safety theorems and deployment. Third, the defect frontier: since softening is coordination loss and the anchor-bracket formalism already identifies corrections exactly from coordination anchors on simple lattices, a defect-anchored field correction that restores Schottky \emph{scale} while provably preserving the (already-correct) \emph{ordering} is the registered next attempt to widen the validated scope beyond lattice constants --- theorem first, then a frozen trial, in that order.
