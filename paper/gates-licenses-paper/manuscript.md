# Softening Is Coordination Loss: Where Foundation Interatomic Potentials Fail, What Can Be Corrected, and What Must Be Refused

> **Date:** 2026-07-13  
> **Deck:** Referenced measurements of foundation-MLIP error across bulk, kinetic, and defect observables in five material classes; a correction with confirmatory out-of-sample validation and machine-checked safety conditions; and the license structure that says when cross-model agreement means anything.  
> **Summary:** Universal potentials get migration barriers nearly right and defect formation energies wrong by half — because softening is coordination loss, not saddle-point error — and we show which of those failures can be provably corrected, which can be refused, and which demand abstention.  
> **Status:** Draft for internal review

---

<div class="callout claim"><strong>Claim.</strong> The systematic error of universal machine-learning interatomic potentials is not uniform: across referenced measurements on halides, perovskites, and alloys, kinetic barriers survive to within ~10 % while defect formation energies collapse by 40–105 % — with cross-material orderings preserved even where magnitudes fail. This structure licenses a narrow, provably-safe correction (class-native lattice constants, validated out-of-sample at 2.4–5×), exact-physics refusals (Born instability of entire ensemble hulls), and honest abstention everywhere else. Which of the three applies is decidable at runtime, and the safety conditions are machine-checked theorems.</div>

<p class="lead">Materials discovery for batteries, solid electrolytes, carbon capture, and lead-free photovoltaics is rate-limited by the same computational bottleneck: the properties that decide whether a candidate works — vacancy formation, migration barriers, defect-mediated stability — live exactly where universal machine-learning interatomic potentials (uMLIPs) are least trained and least accurate. The field knows these models soften the potential-energy surface at under-coordinated sites. What has been missing is resolution: <em>which</em> observables fail, by <em>how much</em> against real references, whether the failure has exploitable structure, and — most practically — a decision procedure that tells a screening pipeline when to trust a prediction, when to repair it, and when to throw it away. This paper reports referenced measurements that answer the first three questions and a validated, theorem-guarded procedure for the fourth.</p>

## The instrument

All measurements share one probe. A deterministic statics core (lattice relaxation by Birch–Murnaghan energy–volume scan; relaxed-ion stress–strain elastic constants at ±0.5 % strain; Born stability as exact arithmetic) extends to defect formation (referenced vacancies, charge-balanced Schottky pairs), to kinetics (climbing-image NEB with relaxed endpoints), and to random solid solutions (exact-count, seed-pinned supercells). Four foundation potentials — CHGNet 0.4.2 and three MACE-MP variants — run through identical code paths on identical cells; a classical LAMMPS leg (2NN-MEAM, native binary driven through the Python module, verified against the standalone executable to 10⁻⁹ GPa) runs the *same cells through the same probe functions* where classical potentials exist. Calibration baselines (21 metals, 6 ionic rocksalts, 5 perovskites, 4 fcc alloys) are measured by the same instrument that gates subjects, so thresholds never inherit a probe mismatch. Reproducibility: a full independent rerun reproduces every verdict, with worst-case raw-value drift of 8×10⁻⁴ (GPU float nondeterminism through finite differences). Every reference value carries a primary citation, a kind (experimental / DFT / derived), and a convention caveat; unfindable values are null, never estimated.

## Result 1 — The softening dichotomy

The sharpest result of this program is a split the softening literature has not resolved. On five rocksalt halides with citation-locked experimental references:

**Kinetic saddle points largely survive.** Cation-vacancy migration barriers from CI-NEB land close to both experiment and published DFT. For LiF, the four models span **0.48–0.68 eV** against an experimental migration enthalpy of **0.66 ± 0.03 eV** (Stoebe & Huggins 1966) and neutral PBE-NEB values of **0.66–0.73 eV**; the best MACE variants sit within ~5 % at the working supercell and within ~10–14 % after finite-size correction (a 3×3×3 convergence study bounds the supercell error at **12–50 meV** — three to eight times smaller than the cross-model spread, so model disagreement is the dominant uncertainty, not the cell).

**Defect formation energetics collapse.** Schottky pair enthalpies — referenced against the conductivity-derived experimental values h_S = 2(E_intrinsic − ΔH_m) for LiF (2.42 eV), LiCl (2.12), LiBr (1.80), LiI (1.34), and NaCl (2.83) — are underestimated by **40–105 % by every model**. The best model reaches barely 60 % of the experimental value on its best compound; the worst predicts *spontaneous* vacancy-pair formation in LiI (−0.08 eV) — a crystal that disassembles itself. The same pathology recurs in the lead-free perovskite class: one model predicts negative B-site vacancy formation in CsGeI₃, and cross-model spreads on neutral vacancy formation run **0.5–1.8 eV**.

**Yet the ordering survives the collapse.** All four models rank the Schottky series LiF > LiCl > LiBr > LiI in the experimental order, even while every magnitude is wrong by half. The size is wrong; the order is right — now measured on defect thermodynamics with experimental anchors, not inferred.

The mechanistic reading is direct. Along a migration path, coordination is approximately conserved: the hopping ion trades neighbors but the saddle configuration is only mildly under-coordinated relative to the minimum, so the systematic softening largely cancels in the barrier *difference*. Defect formation, by contrast, *is* coordination loss — its energy is dominated by exactly the under-coordinated environments the training distributions lack — so the softening appears at full strength. This resolves an apparent tension in screening practice: kinetics-limited screens (conductivity, diffusivity) can tolerate raw uMLIPs far better than stability-limited screens (defect tolerance, degradation), and the difference is not noise but mechanism. At the standard conversion of 100 meV ≈ 50× in rate at room temperature, however, even the surviving barrier spreads of **0.13–0.20 eV** correspond to two to three orders of magnitude of disagreement in predicted ionic conductivity from model choice alone — kinetic screening tolerates raw uMLIPs only under cross-model concordance, not on any single model's word.

## Result 2 — Disagreement has structure, but it is not a universal uncertainty signal

Cross-model dispersion is the cheapest reference-free signal a screening pipeline can compute, and an influential critique (PROBE) reports that raw ensemble disagreement often correlates weakly with true error. Our reference-bound corpora let us measure exactly where that critique lands. The dispersion–error rank correlation is strongly property- and class-dependent: stacking-fault energy ρ = 0.89, vacancy formation 0.61, lattice constants 0.57 pooled — but bulk modulus **0.16** pooled and **−0.63** within fcc metals: for that class–property pair, *higher* disagreement weakly predicts *lower* error. Dispersion gating is an uncertainty statement for some (class, property) pairs and a normality test for others, and nothing in the signal itself tells you which.

Our response is architectural rather than rhetorical: every gate in the pipeline now carries a **license** — `licensed`, `descriptive`, or `anti-correlated` — assigned per (class, property) from reference-bound data under a fixed rule, fail-closed, and updated only from calibration corpora, never from gated subjects. Under the current registry exactly one dispersion gate is licensed as an uncertainty signal (bcc lattice constants, ρ = 0.89, n = 7); bulk-modulus concordance is descriptive program-wide. Refusals that do not depend on any license do the heavy lifting: Born instability is exact physics, and per-model Born failures compose into ensemble statements — if the axis-aligned hull of all models' elastic predictions violates a Born inequality at its favourable endpoint, *every* tensor in the hull is unstable, a machine-checked impossibility certificate that refuses a candidate for the whole ensemble range at once. In our panels these exact-physics refusals correctly rejected a rocksalt LiS hypothesis (all four models: C44 < 0) and, defensibly, cubic CsPbI₃ at 0 K — while per-property calibrated thresholds resolved the false refusals that a single-property proxy had inflicted on known-good subjects (Li₂S antifluorite and the halide electrolyte panel: from 8/8 refused to 4 certified, 2 flagged, 2 marginal under class-native calibration). Because refusal is cheap and final — no later gate can overturn it, a theorem — the pipeline runs its costliest probe only on survivors, cutting panel wall time by 16–42 % with verdicts unchanged; the savings concentrate precisely on the worst physics, since dynamically unstable candidates have the most expensive relaxations.

There is also a hierarchy worth stating plainly: disagreement grows monotonically along bulk → kinetic → defect observables, in both relative and absolute terms (defect-energy disagreement runs 2–34× the bulk-modulus disagreement on the same compounds). The observables that matter most for discovery are the ones the models agree on least — which is the quantitative case for why screening pipelines need verdicts, not just predictions.

## Result 3 — A correction with a validated scope and machine-checked safety

Can any of this be repaired rather than refused? We tested the simplest defensible repair — a multiplicative de-bias learned from same-class reference-bound calibration, applied only when direction evidence is unanimous — through three preregistered stages, and the outcome is a correction whose scope we can state exactly.

Transferring biases *across* chemical classes fails, and fails provably: a bias learned on elemental fcc metals (which underpredict bulk moduli) applied to fcc alloys (which overpredict) *worsened* alloy predictions from 9.1 % to 16.9 % median error. That failure is now a theorem rather than an anecdote — a correction applied against the target's true error direction strictly increases error — and its converse is the safety condition we prove: if all calibration ratios lie strictly on one side of unity, the bias and the target's ratio lie in the calibration hull, and the bias clears a margin cap, then the corrected error is *strictly smaller* than the raw error. The cap is honestly asymmetric: inflation-side corrections are safe when the bias exceeds twice the calibration spread (2s); deflation-side corrections require three times the spread *and* a floor on the bias (the mirrored 2s condition has a kernel-checked counterexample). These are decidable integer statements, machine-checked, with the one harmful correction our exploratory round produced refused by the theorem on two independent grounds.

The confirmatory test used a rule frozen before its evaluation references were even sourced, on eight candidates never seen by any calibration: four alkali halides with primary-source elastic references and four out-of-sample perovskites. The result draws the scope line cleanly. **Class-native lattice-constant corrections transfer decisively: 1.60 % → 0.33 % median error on the ionics (4.8×) and 1.75 % → 0.74 % on the perovskites (2.4×), with the direction gate confident enough to fire on every cell.** Every other property failed the registered criteria — mostly by *abstention* (the gate correctly found no unanimous direction evidence on 12–16 of 16 cells), and where the frozen margin admitted a few applications on stiffness properties, they did not help, consistent with the proven caps being stronger than the frozen one. The registered consequence executed: the correction layer's validated public scope is **same-class lattice constants**, with abstention — provably risk-free — everywhere else until new evidence.

We regard this as a positive result, and the honest shape of the correction problem: lattice constants are where cross-model error direction is unanimous, biases are stable across a chemical class, and thermal-convention offsets are common-mode — so a three-line, fully-auditable correction beats a 4-model ensemble at one quarter of its cost *on that property*, while the same machinery declines to manufacture confidence anywhere it cannot prove it. Combined with Result 1, the division of labor for the five target classes is concrete: rankings and lattice geometry can be corrected; stability energetics must be gated and refused; kinetics demands concordance across independent architectures.

**Toward an independent-architecture basis.** A first cloud cell of the five-architecture program (SevenNet, on the nine campaign candidates) confirms the pipeline end-to-end: 78/78 elastic predictions with an overall Cij MAE of **9.0 GPa** against the locked references under a fixed-lattice protocol (wiring-grade by declaration; the alloy is the hard case at 32 GPa, the perovskites sit at 3–7 GPa). The remaining architecture cells are staged and cost cents; the five-architecture dispersions that replace family spread with independent-error spread are the first registered addition to this record.

**The correction license cannot yet be checked without references — measured.** We tested whether the theorems' load-bearing hypothesis (the target's error ratio lying in the calibration hull) has a reference-free proxy: is a model's *prediction* inside its peers' prediction hull? The answer is a clean null — success rates 73.9 % in-hull vs 73.1 % out (Fisher p = 1.0, 49 confirmatory cells) — while the unknowable oracle condition itself associates strongly (94.4 % vs 61.3 %, p = 0.017), and every cell that satisfied both the proven cap and the in-hull condition succeeded (**12/12 across all rounds** — the theorem's guarantee holding empirically without exception). The theorem picks the right condition; nature declines to reveal it cheaply. Finding a knowable surrogate for hull membership is therefore the registered open problem of the correction program, and until one exists, the fail-closed answer stands: corrections carry the frozen gate, and the license layer reports what that gate cannot see.

## How these results were protected

Every quantitative claim above survived one of three filters, and the paper contains nothing that did not: preregistration with frozen rules and computed (not narrated) success criteria, including one registered kill condition that fired and was executed; kernel-checked proof (Lean 4, zero `sorry`, decidable integer statements over the evidence corpus, with runtime–kernel boundary conventions reconciled); or hostile internal review, whose accepted findings withdrew four earlier claims from this program's record and whose dispositions are public. Exploratory analysis is labeled as such wherever it appears. The registration ledger — what was frozen when, what failed, what executed — is Appendix A; the corrected-record errata are part of the evidence trail.

## Related work

The components of this instrument have prior art; the assembled decision procedure does not, to our knowledge. Ensemble and committee disagreement as an uncertainty proxy is established for bespoke potentials and increasingly for foundation models; PROBE's demonstration that raw disagreement can correlate weakly with error is the direct challenge our license layer answers — by measuring, per class and property, where the proxy holds, and refusing to issue uncertainty semantics where it does not. Conformal calibration supplies distribution-free per-quantity thresholds; our percentile gates are the same statistical object with measured, same-instrument provenance, plus a refusal-first cost ordering conformal methods do not address. Systematic uMLIP softening on under-coordinated configurations is documented (most directly for MPtrj-trained models); our contribution is its mechanistic resolution into a kinetics/thermodynamics dichotomy with experimental anchors on both sides, and the observation that ordering survives magnitude collapse on defect energetics. Proof-carrying certification of MLIP properties is nascent; our Lean layer differs in scope — it certifies the *decision procedure* (refusal finality, threshold-migration consequences, ensemble-hull impossibility, correction-safety caps) rather than the potential, which is what lets a screening pipeline inherit guarantees no single model can offer. Benchmark leaderboards (Matbench Discovery; elastic-property benchmarks showing bulk–shear accuracy asymmetry across ~10⁴ materials) corroborate the property-resolved error structure but operate per-model; the object of study here is the ensemble-plus-verdict system.

## Boundaries

Panels of 4–9 compounds per class demonstrate methodology and mechanism, not survey statistics; every n is stated per material, not per cell. Two of four local architectures share lineage, so dispersions understate independent error until the five-architecture basis lands. Neutral-defect conventions against charged-defect experimental references are stated per value; the Schottky comparison is enthalpy-consistent, and the barrier comparison is anchored on convention-consistent neutral DFT-NEB where it exists. Random-solid-solution cells are statistically cubic only; perovskite references for high-temperature phases meet athermal predictions, and part of the corrected lattice gain absorbs that common-mode offset — the decomposition is registered work. The dispersion metric is undefined at sign-crossing medians and is floored accordingly (one vanadium shear cell in the calibration corpus is a documented pathology, not data). Licenses rest on small-n rank correlations and are treated as fail-closed engineering, not statistics.

## Outlook

Three lines follow directly. First, independence: the five-architecture cloud basis converts every dispersion in this paper from family spread toward independent-error spread at a cost of dollars, and its canary is running. Second, a runtime license for corrections: if prediction-hull membership — knowable without references — predicts correction success, the same fail-closed logic that licenses gates will license repairs, closing the loop between the safety theorems and deployment. Third, the defect frontier: since softening is coordination loss and the anchor-bracket formalism already identifies corrections exactly from coordination anchors on simple lattices, a defect-anchored field correction that restores Schottky *scale* while provably preserving the (already-correct) *ordering* is the registered next attempt to widen the validated scope beyond lattice constants — theorem first, then a frozen trial, in that order.
