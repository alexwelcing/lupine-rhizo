# China-Lab uMLIPs: State of the Art and Correction Practice

**Literature cutoff:** 20 July 2026
**Program:** Lupine MLIP runtime-correction program
**Provenance rule:** Numerical claims below are quoted only from accessed primary papers, publisher full text, or Lupine's content-addressed campaign reports. `DIRECT` means primary-literature evidence; `LUPINE` means an internal measured result; `INFERENCE` is a proposed interpretation; `UNACCESSED` marks evidence that was not available in sufficient detail. A model's training-set error is never relabeled here as adsorption, surface, or barrier accuracy.

## Executive conclusion

The Chinese Deep Potential/OpenLAM ecosystem has moved beyond a single universal checkpoint toward a model-development stack: invariant local descriptors (DPA-1), heterogeneous multi-task representations and task heads (DPA-2), line-graph-series message passing with scalable dataset encodings (DPA3), explicit electrostatic physics (DPLR), ensemble model deviation (DeePMD-kit/DP-GEN), and application-local fine-tuning plus distillation. The strongest directly relevant catalysis result is not a claim of reliable zero-shot universality: Nankai University's CLAM study reports a roughly **0.3 eV** initial Ru(0001) barrier error and **0.230 eV** zero-DFT-optimization adsorption MAE, then trades speed for accuracy through repeated local DFT fine-tuning, reaching adsorption MAEs of **0.028 eV** after two loops and **0.012 eV** after three.

No accessed DPA, MatterSim, OpenLAM, FastCHGNet, or CLAM paper establishes a calibrated production policy that accepts or abstains at each MD/optimization step under a validated error bound. DP-GEN's lower/upper model-deviation thresholds and MatterSim's ensemble monitor are documented **active-learning acquisition rules**, not a safety guarantee. This distinction is central for Lupine: the literature supports correction and acquisition, but does not remove the need to calibrate a task-specific runtime gate.

Lupine's own Round-4 measurements are more severe than the broad literature benchmark: migration-barrier MAEs are **135.0–242.5 meV** over 26–29 completed paths, and adsorption holdout MAEs are **0.69–4.27 eV** before correction. All models fail their registered gates. These results agree in direction with the published systematic-softening diagnosis, but the large adsorption errors are additionally consistent with a distribution/physics mismatch (large PBE+D2 adsorbates on slabs versus mainly bulk-relaxation pretraining), not a globally correctable scalar bias.

## Comparator: what “different from MACE/CHGNet” means

- **MACE** uses E(3)-equivariant message passing with explicitly higher body-order messages. Its MP foundation line was trained on Materials Project relaxation trajectories (MPtrj). This creates a strong geometric inductive bias, but the pretraining distribution remains dominated by inorganic bulk relaxations.
- **CHGNet** is an invariant atom/bond/angle graph network that jointly predicts energy, forces, stress, and magnetic moments, using magnetic moments as charge-related supervision. The canonical pretrained model also uses the approximately **1.58 million-frame MPtrj** distribution.
- Therefore, architecture and data coverage must be separated. DPA-2 reports that MPtrj-trained DPA-2 and MACE-MP-0 are comparable, while multi-task DPA-2 improves sharply; the authors explicitly attribute the principal gain to multi-task training rather than architecture alone [2].

## Published softening evidence and its correction

The direct softening diagnosis is from Deng et al. [12] (Berkeley/LBNL, therefore a comparator rather than China-lab evidence). Across M3GNet, CHGNet, and MACE-MP-0, they report systematic underprediction for surfaces, defects, mixing energetics, phonons, ion migration and high-energy configurations. Key accessed values are:

- surface-energy benchmark: MACE MAE **0.032 eV/Å²**; all three models mostly underestimate surface energies;
- ion migration: **477** barrier differences, with MACE **0.30 eV**, CHGNet **0.39 eV**, M3GNet **0.49 eV** MAE and predominantly negative signed errors;
- high-energy WBM test: over **90%** of 1,000 compounds have softening scale below 1 for all three models;
- scalar correction: CHGNet on Li2B3PO8 moves from softening scale/force MAE **0.859 / 0.190 eV/Å** to **0.965 / 0.166 eV/Å** using one high-energy configuration and a scalar energy layer; full ten-structure fine-tuning reaches **0.125 eV/Å**;
- across the three models, scalar-corrected force MAEs move from **0.220/0.190/0.176** to **0.184/0.162/0.155 eV/Å** for M3GNet/CHGNet/MACE.

This is evidence that some OOD error is low-dimensional and correctable. It is not evidence that all underbinding is a scalar. Lupine's Z3 holdout directly refutes that extrapolation for large adsorption systems: a six-point global/family/size correction made every model worse.

## Comparison with Lupine's measured MACE/CHGNet behavior

### Z1 migration barriers (`LUPINE`)

The locked 30-chemistry LiTraj/nebDFT2k panel gives float64 CI-NEB MAEs:

| Model | MAE | Completed paths | Registered gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MACE-MPA-0-medium | **135.0 meV** | 28/30 | 40 meV — fail |
| MACE-MP-small | **151.9 meV** | 26/30 | fail |
| MACE-MP-medium | **174.7 meV** | 29/30 | fail |
| CHGNet | **242.5 meV** | 28/30 | fail |

The best Lupine number is lower than the broad published MACE 0.30 eV MAE, but still 3.4× above Lupine's threshold. The float32 and float64 verdicts agree to 0.1 meV in aggregate, ruling out calculator precision as the explanation. MACE-MP-small's float64 signed distribution is mixed (17/26 negative, mean −23.7 meV), so “all paths underpredict” must not be claimed for the corrected chain. The aggregate underprediction direction is nevertheless consistent with the published curvature-softening mechanism.

### Z3 adsorption (`LUPINE`)

On 20 CatBench confirmatory holdouts using PBE+D2 references:

| Model | Raw holdout MAE | Full-panel signed bias |
|---|---:|---:|
| CHGNet | **0.69 eV** | **+1.13 eV** |
| MACE-MP-medium | **2.11 eV** | **+3.42 eV** |
| MACE-MP-small | **3.24 eV** | **+4.28 eV** |
| MACE-MPA-0-medium | **4.27 eV** | **+5.29 eV** |

Positive signed error is underbinding. Per-candidate error reaches **+25.6 eV** and grows with adsorbate size/family. A six-training/six-validation Δ-correction worsens every holdout: CHGNet **0.69→2.27 eV**, MACE-MP-medium **2.11→5.01**, MACE-MP-small **3.24→5.00**, MACE-MPA-0-medium **4.27→5.91**. This differs from the one-scalar WBM correction: the Z3 error is structured by interface, family, size and likely missing dispersion/domain physics.

CLAM is the closest literature analogue. Its catalyst-local workflow starts at 0.230 eV adsorption MAE and ≈0.3 eV barrier error, then reaches chemical accuracy only by purchasing DFT labels and losing speed. Lupine's larger raw adsorption errors imply that the first decision should be **abstention/domain routing**, not a tiny blind residual fit.

## What is and is not published runtime gating

1. **Published:** DP-GEN thresholds ensemble force disagreement to choose candidate frames for DFT labelling; its Cu example uses 0.05 and 0.20 eV/Å trust bounds.
2. **Published:** MatterSim selects high-uncertainty frames with an ensemble and actively expands training data.
3. **Published:** DPA-2 distillation compares teacher/student predictions during application-conditioned MD to grow the student data.
4. **Not established:** a universal mapping from disagreement to actual adsorption/barrier error.
5. **Not established:** calibrated precision/recall for an accept/abstain threshold on slabs, interfaces or NEB paths.
6. **Not established:** a production policy that falls back to DFT while preserving a certified error ceiling.

Thus Lupine may reuse these mechanisms, but must calibrate and formally record its own gate.

## Program hypotheses

1. **Path-aware correction beats minimum-only correction.** `INFERENCE` Train on initial/final states plus selected intermediate/saddle images, then measure the locked Z1 barrier slope and signed error. CLAM's 8-image correction and Lupine's Z1 miss predict that minima-only acquisition will leave curvature error unresolved.

2. **Use two gates, not one uncertainty scalar.** `INFERENCE` A committee-disagreement gate should detect epistemic/OOD regions, while a separate physics/applicability gate flags long-range charge separation, polar slabs, spin-sensitive surfaces and dispersion-dominated large adsorbates. DPLR shows locality bias can be shared by all ensemble members; Z3 shows structured interface error that a scalar committee may not expose.

3. **Training distribution should be tested as an intervention.** `INFERENCE` Run DPA-1-OC2M, multi-task DPA-2/DPA3, and a CLAM checkpoint on the exact frozen Z1/Z3 panels. Comparing MPtrj-only DPA-2 with multi-task DPA-2 and MACE controls architecture versus data coverage; no aggregate foundation benchmark can substitute for this paired test.

4. **Preregister an accuracy–DFT-cost frontier.** `INFERENCE` Mirror CLAM's correction ladder: zero DFT, endpoints, one path/image batch, and final DFT refinement. Record adsorption/barrier MAE, gate coverage, abstention rate and speedup at each rung. A correction is useful only if it improves held-out error without hiding its DFT cost.

5. **Distillation comes after correction and gate calibration.** `INFERENCE` If a DPA-2/teacher model is corrected, distill against application-space trajectories only after checking that the student preserves adsorption differences, barrier curvature, signed bias and gate decisions—not just frame-level energy/force RMSE.
