# Runtime correction mechanisms for universal/foundation MLIPs (2023–2026)

**Scope.** Primary literature on inference-time add-ons, residual/delta stacks, targeted fine-tuning, and correction loops around universal/foundation machine-learned interatomic potentials (uMLIPs). The search emphasized US/EU work but retains one 2025 China-led CHGNet study because it is the clearest published demonstration of a corrected foundation potential operating *inside both NEB and MD* [R5].

**Project context (not an external literature claim).** The task brief reports a structured adsorption underbinding field of −1 to +26 eV for which a six-point delta fit failed (Z3), and barrier underprediction by factors of 3.4–6 (Z1). These internal measurements are used only to rank hypotheses below; they are not treated as published evidence.

**Provenance rule.** Numerical claims below come from the cited primary papers. “Held-out” means that the paper explicitly identifies excluded/test configurations. “In-loop” means the corrected model generated forces/energies during relaxation, global optimization, NEB, or MD, rather than merely rescoring fixed structures. Where a paper does not report an isolated correction gain, exact label count, or held-out metric, the table says so.

**Terminology/access note.** I could not identify a verifiable 2023–2026 uMLIP paper whose method is formally named **qPP**. I therefore do **not** equate that label with a specific publication. The “qPP-style” row below covers the verifiable nearby mechanism—adding an explicit, differentiable physics term (D3 dispersion) to a frozen uMLIP at evaluation time [R6].

## Executive result

The strongest small-budget result is a *one-scalar* correction of systematic PES softening: one high-energy DFT configuration reduced CHGNet force MAE from 0.190 to 0.166 eV Å⁻¹ on excluded forces; ten configurations reduced it to 0.125 eV Å⁻¹ [R1]. This is directly relevant to Z1-like multiplicative barrier softening but is not evidence that one scalar can repair Z3’s sign-changing, −1 to +26 eV adsorption field.

For errors that change with local chemistry or bonding motif, the literature supports either (i) targeted fine-tuning with tens to thousands of relevant configurations [R2, R5, R7–R9], or (ii) a local residual model updated from DFT points acquired by the actual search [R3, R4]. MatterSim transferred to hybrid-functional liquid water with 30 configurations and then ran MD [R8], while MACE-MP-0 reached first-principles-quality ice NPT observables with 50–100 structures [R9]. The cleanest barrier result used 3,115 transition-state configurations (2,784 in the model-development pool plus a separate 331-configuration test set) and cut held-out barrier MAE from 0.23 to 0.09 eV; the corrected CHGNet then ran inside NEB and 200 ps MD [R5]. That is strong evidence for mechanism efficacy, but not for a six-point budget.

## Mechanism notes

### 1. Low-rank/global correction: one scalar can remove a common softening mode

Deng et al. measured force softening across 1,000 WBM compounds, each sampled with ten 1,000 K MD states; more than 90% had fitted force slopes below one for M3GNet, CHGNet, and MACE-MP-0 [R1]. Their correction is unusually constrained: a scalar multiplies total energy, so forces scale by the same scalar [R1]. On Li₂B₃PO₈, this raised the force-slope estimate from 0.859 to 0.965 and reduced excluded-force MAE from 0.190 to 0.166 eV Å⁻¹ using one high-energy configuration; ten full-model labels reached 0.125 eV Å⁻¹ [R1].

This is a useful *diagnostic before fitting*: if Z1 residual barriers collapse under a single multiplicative slope, a one-parameter correction is supported by [R1]. If residuals retain material-, path-, or environment-dependent sign structure, [R1] supplies no evidence that more scalar labels will solve it.

### 2. Property- and chemistry-targeted fine-tuning

Frozen transfer learning preserves the pretrained representation and limits adaptation to selected late layers [R2]. In H₂/Cu, the MACE-MP-f4 model used only a 10% subset of a 4,230-structure reactive database yet beat a bespoke full-data MACE on the paper’s reported test energy and force errors [R2]. Crucially, this was not post-hoc scoring: the corrected model generated the scattering trajectories used to compute sticking probabilities [R2].

The 2025 cross-architecture study extended the same conclusion beyond MACE: MACE, GRACE, SevenNet, MatterSim, and ORB all reached 0.02–0.07 eV Å⁻¹ force error after system-specific fine-tuning of 2,000-frame AIMD datasets and then ran 2–10 ns production MD [R7]. The evidence supports “fine-tune the target chemistry/dynamics” more strongly than “choose one universally superior backbone” [R7].

Two studies establish a smaller in-loop budget bracket. MatterSim fine-tuned on 30 rev-PBE0-D3 water configurations matched a model trained from scratch on 900 configurations for liquid structure and predicted a diffusion coefficient within 20% of experiment [R8]. MACE-MP-0 fine-tuned on 50–100 ice structures reached sub-kJ mol⁻¹ and sub-1% finite-temperature agreement with DFT and ran NPT simulations; at RPA level, noisy forces destabilized NPT even though NVT remained usable, directly illustrating why trajectory stability must be an acceptance test [R9].

### 3. Transition-state-targeted fine-tuning is the clearest barrier remedy

Lian et al. generated transition states with pretrained CHGNet-NEB, labeled 3,115 configurations by static DFT, and fine-tuned CHGNet on the resulting energies, forces, and stresses [R5]. On a separate 331-configuration test set, barrier MAE fell from 0.23 to 0.09 eV and R² rose from 0.94 to 0.98 [R5]. The corrected model then drove NEB and long MD, recovering LATP activation energy and diffusion substantially closer to AIMD than the pretrained model [R5].

The caveat matters: the “DFT barrier references” for the broad train/test comparison were single-point DFT energies at CHGNet-predicted transition states, not independent fully relaxed DFT-NEB pathways [R5]. The smaller LTP example did compare a corrected CHGNet-NEB barrier of 0.40 eV to a DFT-NEB value of 0.38 eV [R5].

### 4. Residual Δ models can change bonding motifs without modifying the foundation model

Christiansen and Hammer attach a GPR correction directly to CHGNet’s learned atomic embeddings [R3]. In each GOFEE iteration, the search relaxes candidates under CHGNet+Δ, selects one candidate, runs one single-point DFT calculation, and updates the residual [R3]. For sulfur overlayers, the corrected search removed bare-CHGNet’s spurious preference for sulfur dimers and found DFT-lower motifs, including 1.30 eV and 2.29 eV improvements for compared S₇/Ag(111) and S₇/Au(111) structures [R3].

This is the closest published analogue to a structured adsorption residual field in the task brief. However, [R3] does not provide a held-out adsorption-energy error or a fixed six-label test, so it cannot justify expecting success at a six-point budget.

### 5. Active Δ learning puts correction acquisition inside the scientific loop

Pitfield et al. use a local SOAP-GPR Δ model on three frozen uMLIP priors and let the global-search algorithm select DFT labels online [R4]. Replica exchange remained above 80% success for the largest tested [Ag₂S] clusters, while simpler search strategies degraded sharply; for [Ag₂S]₁₂, GOFEE and basin hopping each found the solution in only one of 25 repeats [R4]. For the larger Ag(100) sulfur reconstruction, none of the uncorrected uMLIPs predicted the DFT global minimum, but the active Δ searches eventually found it with all three priors [R4].

This supports coupling the correction to the *sampling policy*, not merely choosing a richer residual model. It does not establish stability in MD or generalization to held-out reaction paths [R4].

### 6. Add known missing physics rather than asking a residual learner to rediscover it

Sauer et al. add D3 energy and forces to six PBE-trained foundation MLIPs and use the sums to optimize 336 van der Waals heterobilayers [R6]. The correction requires no new labels and gives useful absolute PBE-D3 structural accuracy for several backbones [R6]. Because [R6] omits an uncorrected control, it cannot separate the numerical gain due to D3 from the baseline model quality. The mechanism is therefore compelling when the missing interaction is known in advance, but the paper is not evidence for a generic adsorption/barrier correction.

## Program hypotheses

1. **Fit a rank-1 softening diagnostic before any flexible delta.** Regress Z1 reference forces or barrier increments against foundation-model values with one global slope and test it under leave-one-path/material-out validation. A one-configuration scalar corrected a meaningful portion of CHGNet force softening in [R1]; failure of this diagnostic would falsify the cheapest mechanism before spending more DFT.

2. **For Z1, acquire transition-state images rather than more minima.** The best direct barrier evidence trained on uMLIP-generated transition-state configurations and reduced separate-test barrier MAE from 0.23 to 0.09 eV before running corrected NEB/MD [R5]. A small-budget campaign should therefore allocate labels across endpoints, shoulders, and saddle neighborhoods, not six equilibrium structures; 30 and 50 configurations are empirically grounded first escalation budgets from successful MD transfer studies [R8, R9], though neither is barrier evidence.

3. **For Z3, replace a global six-point delta with an environment-local residual plus abstention.** Embedding-GPR and SOAP-GPR residuals changed wrong sulfur bonding motifs and recovered DFT global minima while leaving the uMLIP frozen [R3, R4]. Pre-register leave-one-adsorbate and leave-one-surface-family tests; if uncertainty is high, request a DFT label rather than applying a large extrapolated correction.

4. **Make label acquisition loop-aware.** Active Δ learning succeeded when DFT points were selected from structures the optimizer actually visited, and search strategy materially changed success [R4]. For NEB, query the image with the largest committee/GPR uncertainty or largest predicted correction; for MD, query only after stability and novelty guards. This hypothesis is supported for global optimization by [R4] and remains to be validated for the Z1/Z3 loops.

5. **Use explicit physics only for identified missing terms, then learn the residual.** D3 can be added with zero fitting labels and differentiable forces during relaxation [R6]. For adsorption systems with diagnosed dispersion error, evaluate `uMLIP + D3 + local Δ`; do not treat D3 as a generic remedy for Z3-scale chemisorption errors because [R6] did not test that regime.

## Evidence gaps that should constrain claims

- No verified paper here demonstrates repair of a sign-changing **−1 to +26 eV** adsorption residual with six or fewer DFT labels.
- The one-label result is a global scalar correction on excluded force components, not a held-out adsorption or barrier benchmark [R1].
- The embedding-based Δ paper reports lower DFT energies and corrected motifs but no held-out MAE and no fixed total label count [R3].
- The active Δ paper reports optimization success curves, not a transferable fit-point threshold or MD/NEB stability [R4].
- The D3 benchmark reports absolute accuracy of corrected models but no uncorrected control, so an isolated D3 accuracy delta is unavailable [R6].
- The strongest in-loop barrier result used thousands—not single-digit numbers—of transition-state labels [R5].
